The short version
  • Sedona trails are short, beautiful and crowded. Start by 7 am or go after 3 pm, and use the free Sedona Shuttle for Cathedral Rock, Dry Creek (Devil’s Bridge) and Soldier Pass; those trailheads are closed to cars at peak times.
  • Best first hikes: Bell Rock Pathway (easy), Fay Canyon (easy), Doe Mountain (moderate, best view for effort), Cathedral Rock (short, steep scramble), West Fork of Oak Creek (shady, water, long).
  • Buy a Red Rock Pass ($5 per day) or use an America the Beautiful pass at most trailheads.
  • Want red rock without the crowds? Sycamore Canyon, 30 minutes west, is our guides’ answer.

Before you set off

  • Parking is the hard part. The Cathedral Rock, Dry Creek Vista (Devil’s Bridge) and Soldier Pass trailheads are served by the free Sedona Shuttle from park-and-ride lots (Thursday to Sunday for most of the year, more days in spring), and are closed to private vehicles on shuttle days. Check sedonashuttle.com. Other trailheads fill by 8 am in spring and autumn.
  • Red Rock Pass. $5 a day or $15 a week at trailhead machines; an America the Beautiful pass also works at most sites.
  • Heat and exposure. Sedona is 4,300 ft, hotter than Flagstaff, with little shade on the slickrock. Summer hikes are dawn hikes.
  • Cairns, not trails. Several routes cross bare rock and are marked only by rock cairns or painted blazes. If you have not seen a cairn for 5 minutes, go back to the last one.

If you want red rock without the Sedona crowds

Thirty minutes west of Sedona, past Cottonwood and Clarkdale, Sycamore Canyon's Parsons Trail follows a spring-fed creek between red walls as high as anything in Sedona, with a fraction of the people. In autumn the cottonwoods along Oak Creek and in Sycamore Canyon turn gold; our fall colour post has timing.
