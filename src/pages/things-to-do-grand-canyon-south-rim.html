The short version
  • With one day: Mather Point early, the Rim Trail through the Village, a short walk below the rim, then Desert View Drive to the Watchtower for the afternoon light.
  • The East Rim overlooks (Grandview, Moran, Lipan, Navajo Point) are the best views in the park and the ones most day-trippers never reach.
  • Sunset from Hopi, Mohave or Lipan Point is the finest free thing in Arizona.
  • Skip: driving between the Village car parks in summer. Walk or take the free shuttles.

The overlooks, in the order we would do them

  1. Mather Point. The first view, five minutes from the Visitor Center. Go before 9 am or after 4 pm; at midday it is a scrum. It is still worth it.
  2. The Rim Trail, Mather Point to the Village (2.5 miles, flat, paved). Yavapai Point and the Geology Museum (look at the relief model, then look at the real thing), then Trail of Time, where every metre is a million years. Shuttles bring you back.
  3. Grand Canyon Village. El Tovar (1905), Hopi House, Lookout Studio and Kolb Studio: Mary Colter's stone buildings on the very edge. Have a coffee on the El Tovar porch. Watch the mule trains come up Bright Angel.
  4. Hermit Road overlooks (west). Hopi Point for sunset, Mohave for the river and Hermit Rapid, The Abyss for vertigo. Private cars are banned March to November; take the red shuttle or walk the Rim Trail.
  5. Desert View Drive (east, 25 miles). Grandview Point (the widest view), Moran Point (the red layers Thomas Moran painted), Lipan Point (the river, the Unkar Delta, the best sunset on the East Rim), Navajo Point and the Desert View Watchtower, Colter's 70-ft stone tower with Hopi murals inside. Climb it.

Walk below the rim, even a little

The canyon changes the moment you drop below the edge. Two easy tasters: Bright Angel Trail to the first tunnel (10 minutes down) and South Kaibab Trail to Ooh Aah Point (0.9 miles down, 600 ft, 45 minutes to an hour back up). Carry water, start early in summer, and remember that down is optional and up is mandatory. Our day hikes guide covers the longer options.

Things worth your time

  • Ranger programmes. Free, daily, listed at the Visitor Center. The geology and condor talks are excellent.
  • The condors. California condors (9-ft wingspan) roost near the Village and Lookout Studio. Ask a ranger or your guide where they have been this week.
  • Tusayan Ruin and Museum on Desert View Drive: an 800-year-old Ancestral Puebloan village, usually empty.
  • Sunset. Hopi Point is the classic (and crowded); Lipan Point faces the right way and has a tenth of the people. Why we think sunset is non-negotiable.
  • Stargazing. The park is an International Dark Sky Park. Even from the Village car park the Milky Way is astonishing on a moonless night.
  • The bike paths. The Greenway Trail from the Visitor Center to Hermit Road is paved and car-free. Our e-bike and hike day rides it.

Things to skip (or at least not pay for)

  • Driving lot to lot in the Village in summer. Park once, walk and use the shuttles.
  • The IMAX in Tusayan, unless it is raining. The real thing is 10 minutes away.
  • Trying to "see the river" from Mather Point. You can glimpse it; go to Lipan, Mohave or Desert View for a proper look.
