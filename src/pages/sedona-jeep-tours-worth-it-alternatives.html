The short version
  • A Sedona Jeep tour is a 1.5 to 3-hour open-air ride on rough forest roads with a driver-guide, big views and a lot of bouncing. Several companies run them; Pink is the famous one.
  • Worth it if: you have young kids, limited mobility, two hours to spare, or you simply want the ride. Not a substitute for hiking or for a full day out.
  • Alternatives: the same viewpoints on foot, the scenic byways in your own car for free, or a private guided day that pairs Sedona with Oak Creek Canyon or the Grand Canyon.
  • We are not a Jeep company. We are a Flagstaff guide company, and we would rather you book the right thing than ours.

What you actually get on a Jeep tour

Most Sedona Jeep tours follow a handful of US Forest Service roads that private cars cannot (or should not) drive: Broken Arrow (slickrock, Submarine Rock, Chicken Point: the classic), Schnebly Hill Road (a long climb with views over the whole valley), Soldier Pass (the sinkhole and the Seven Sacred Pools) and the Diamondback Gulch area west of town. The vehicle is a modified open-sided Jeep carrying 6 to 10 guests; the driver narrates the geology and the Hollywood history; there are two or three photo stops. Rides run 1.5 to 3 hours, and the rough-road sections are genuinely rough: expect to hold on.

When it is the right choice

  • Young children. The bouncing is the attraction. A four-year-old who will not hike a mile will talk about the Jeep for a year.
  • Limited mobility. You see slickrock country you could not otherwise reach without walking.
  • Time. If Sedona is a half-day stop between Phoenix and the Grand Canyon, a two-hour ride is efficient.
  • You want the ride. That is a perfectly good reason.

When it is not

  • You came to hike. Jeep tours stop at viewpoints; they do not walk you anywhere. Two hours in a Jeep is two hours you could have spent on Doe Mountain or in West Fork. Our Sedona hiking guide.
  • You want quiet. The popular roads carry a steady stream of vehicles in spring and autumn, and the stops are shared.
  • Dust, wind and sun bother you. It is an open vehicle on dirt roads in a desert. Bring a buff and sunglasses.
  • You expected a full day. Most rides are under three hours. Price per hour is high.

If you book a Jeep tour anyway

  • Book the first morning slot: cooler, emptier roads, better light on the red rock.
  • Broken Arrow is the ride to pick if you only do one.
  • Ask the group size; some operators run smaller vehicles.
  • Pair it with a hike or a scenic drive the same day so Sedona is not just two hours in a vehicle.
