The short version
  • "Grand Canyon rafting" covers three very different things: a calm half-day float from Glen Canyon Dam to Lees Ferry, a one-day whitewater run on Hualapai land at the far west end, and the classic 3 to 18-day expeditions through the whole canyon.
  • The multi-day trips are the trip of a lifetime and book up a year or more ahead. The half-day float can be booked the same week.
  • None of them show you what the rim shows you, and the rim shows you nothing of the river. Do both if you can.
  • We run the rim side: small-group day tours, sunset tours and guided hikes from Flagstaff, 2 hours from Page and Lees Ferry.

If you only have a day: float or rim?

Different experiences for different people. The Glen Canyon float is gentle, beautiful and feels like a secret; the sandstone walls are the same Navajo Sandstone you see at Antelope Canyon, and you drift under Horseshoe Bend looking up at the people who drove there. But it is not the Grand Canyon proper, and you will not see the mile-deep layered views. For that you want to be on the South Rim, ideally with the afternoon light moving across the Palisades from Lipan Point or Desert View.

If you want to be on the water near Lees Ferry without a commercial float, our guides have written up two self-powered options: kayaking Horseshoe Bend from below with a backhaul and Waterholes Canyon by kayak.

A plan that does both

  1. Day 1: Grand Canyon day tour from Flagstaff. See the canyon from above, learn the geology, end at Desert View.
  2. Day 2: drive to Page (2 hours; stop at the dam overlook), take the half-day float in the morning, Horseshoe Bend from the top at sunset.

Before you book a multi-day trip

  • Commercial outfitters are licensed by the National Park Service; the park website lists them all. Motor trips are shorter and smoother; oar and dory trips are quieter and longer.
  • Summer is hot (often 100°F+ in the inner gorge) and busiest. April to May and September to October are the sweet spots.
  • Trips that start or end at Phantom Ranch involve hiking the Bright Angel Trail with a daypack: 9.5 miles and 4,400 ft. Train for it. Our rim-to-river post explains what that hike is really like.
  • Non-commercial (private) permits are a weighted lottery run each February for the following year.
